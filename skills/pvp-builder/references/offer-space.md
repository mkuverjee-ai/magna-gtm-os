# Offer space: how to go wide before going narrow

A PVP (an information gift) is one kind of offer. It is not the only one, and it is often not the one
that gets the reply. Measured on one programme selling post-purchase software to e-commerce brands: two
rounds of information gifts (a peer benchmark, then a profit forecast) both pointed the buyer at their
incumbent or read as unbelievable. The ideas that moved the conversation came from other cells of this
grid: a risk-free switch, a mockup of their own checkout, a free claims desk for peak, data only the
vendor had.

Use this file in phase 4 (DIVERGE). The point is quantity and range first. Gates come later.

---

## The grid: what you give x when it lands

| What you give | In email 1 (exists at send) | Named in email 1, delivered on yes | After a call / in a trial |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Information** (a fact, a pattern, a benchmark) | peer benchmark, the trigger list, their own data read back with an outside lens | the full report | the forecast with their real numbers |
| **An artifact of THEIR thing** (built for them, their brand on it) | their checkout / site / ad / page mocked up with the product on it | the editable file, a hosted page | the live version |
| **A service slice** (we do one job for them) | "we already did X for you" (e.g. remade 5 ads) | we take one job off them for a set period | full onboarding |
| **Risk reversal / money** | a guarantee stated in the email | first month at cost, pay-the-difference guarantee, contract buyout | a paid pilot with an exit |
| **Access** | data only you have (network aggregates), an expert's view | an introduction, a seat at an event, early access | a reference customer call |
| **Time / timing** | a deadline that is real (peak, regulation, renewal) | a slot before the deadline | a "live before X date" commitment |

Every candidate list must touch **at least four rows**. If all your ideas sit in the Information row,
you have not diverged yet.

## The lenses (one subagent each in phase 4)

Give each lens subagent the context pack, the segment, the asset inventory, this file and the offer
library below. Ask for 5+ raw ideas each, no self-censoring, one line each plus "what it would take".

1. **Information gift (classic PVP).** What outside fact would they forward?
2. **Build their thing.** What could we mock up, remake or pre-build with their brand on it, from what is
   public (their site HTML, their ads, their catalogue, a recorded session)?
3. **Risk reversal.** What would make saying yes cost them nothing? Free first month, at-cost first month,
   pay-the-difference, buyout of the incumbent's remaining contract, money-back, "we eat the downside".
4. **Done-for-you slice.** Which single job could you take off them, for free, for a fixed period,
   without them ripping anything out?
5. **Data only you have.** What aggregate across your own customers would this buyer pay to
   see? (Network benchmarks, loss maps, rate tables. Aggregated, n>=10, nothing identifying.)
6. **Incumbent killer.** What can you send that the buyer's current vendor structurally cannot? If the
   incumbent could send it tomorrow, it belongs in another lens.
7. **Timing.** What real date makes this matter now (season, regulation, contract end, launch), and what
   would we hand them before that date?
8. **Borrowed from the library.** Which offer from the library below fits, translated to this buyer?
9. **The buyer's 10-second yes.** Run as the buyer persona (with real objections from calls/replies): "What
   would you reply yes to without thinking? What would you pay for?"
10. **Unlimited budget, then shrink.** If you could spend $50k to win this one account, what would they
    do? Now find the version that costs $50, or the version worth doing for the top 20 accounts only.

## Combine, don't just rank

The best result is usually a **stack**, not a single idea: one thing that earns attention (a gift or
artifact), one thing that removes the risk of saying yes (an offer), and one obvious next step.

> Example: email 1 = their own cart, mocked up with coverage on it (artifact) ->
> offer = "keep <incumbent> for returns, we take lost-parcel claims free until 15 Jan" (service slice + timing) ->
> next step = a 2-week 50/50 checkout test, they keep the data (risk reversal).

## The offer library (seeds from real campaigns, names removed)

Real offers, what made them work, and what is known about results. Add to this every time you test an
offer (and log it with `gtm.py log-intel`). A seed is a starting point to translate, not a template to paste.

| Seller type | Offer | Cell | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| Payments / fintech | Users of a competitor get their first month at cost to test side by side | Risk reversal | Lets a switcher prove the difference on their own numbers |
| Agency | The prospect's own report (their ads, their site, their data) built automatically and attached to the reply | Artifact / information | Cheap per prospect; bridges straight to "we'd fix this for you" |
| Agency | Competitors' best work remade for the prospect | Build their thing | Capability gift; gate it to top-tier accounts to match delivery capacity |
| Services firm | A small, unconditional thank-you for a short proof call | Money | When the most common positive reply is "send me the proof", the call is the proof |
| Data platform | A plain "yes" gets the event registration link automatically | Access | The event is the CTA; no meeting ask |
| Data provider | Send a sample of your data, get a sample output back | Service slice | The buyer supplies the input, so the output is theirs |
| B2B services | Something built for the prospect's company before the first call | Build their thing | It exists at send time, so the offer is concrete, not a promise |
| B2B services | Week one of work for the hire they're recruiting for | Information + build | Showing how it was built moved skeptical buyers from "not now" to reply |
| Vendor selling via partners | A partner's list of target accounts outside their own book, with a written no-direct-sell rule | Information + risk reversal | The written rule is what made partners forward it internally |
| Software | Buy out the remaining months of a top-tier account's competitor contract | Risk reversal | Untested; the seller must be willing to underwrite it |

## Traps found the hard way

- **The forecast trap.** "I can give you a profit forecast" from a stranger is not believable. A forecast is a
  post-yes asset, not a hook.
- **Sending them to the incumbent.** A neutral benchmark of "how peers solved this" sends the buyer to whatever
  their current vendor sells. Run the incumbent test (G9) on every gift.
- **Asking for their private numbers as the opener.** "What's your rev share?" got nothing when tested. A question
  only works if answering it costs them nothing and gets them something.
- **Data-first thinking.** "No field, no PVP" applies to every *claim* inside a gift. It does not apply to
  *offers*: a guarantee or a free service slice needs an internal commitment, not a database field.
