# Cold Email Copywriting Playbook

Load `company/context.md` for positioning and proof before writing.

## The 4-Part Email Anatomy

| Component | Job | Target |
|-----------|-----|--------|
| **Subject Line** | Get the email opened | 3-5 words, looks internal |
| **Preview/First Line** | Get them to read | Observation, pain, or industry hook |
| **Value Prop** | Answer "why should I care?" | 2-3 sentences max |
| **CTA** | Get a response | Low-friction, interest-based |

---

## 1. Subject Lines

**One job:** Get the email opened. You are not selling anything in the subject line.

**4 Rules:**
- **Short** — 3-5 words max (mobile-friendly)
- **Clear** — Not clickbait (signals relevance)
- **Internal-looking** — Looks like a colleague sent it (all lowercase, no spammy punctuation)
- **Relevant** — Tied to their world (teases value or insight)

**Good vs Bad:**

| Good | Bad |
|------|-----|
| benefits consulting | Last chance! |
| your audit | Grow your revenue 10x |
| potential lead | Partnership opportunity |
| leads for {{company}} | Free trial offer |
| question about {{product}} | EXCLUSIVE DEAL INSIDE |
| hiring reps | {{Company}} x {{Company}} |

**3 Proven Formulas:**
1. **Observation > Curiosity** — `noticed {{trigger}} at {{company}}`
2. **Problem > Conversation** — `hiring reps` / `missing meetings`
3. **Value > Relevance** — `cut ramp time in half` / `demo conversion rates`

---

## 2. Preview / First Lines

### Type 1: Observation (General triggers)
Call out something noticed without stating why.
> "Hey John, saw you offer a free audit on your site."

Works because it could mean: potential customer, question, or research.

### Type 2: Pain (Specific triggers)
Reference a pain point you are confident they have.
- BAD: "Noticed you're hiring a BDR." (generic)
- GOOD: "Hey John, here is a top of funnel outbound playbook. With {{company}} hiring for a BDR I assume generating top of funnel is a priority."

### Type 3: Industry (Enterprise & traditional)
Hot topic, regulation, or industry event.
- **Popular news:** Subject: `crowdstrike outage` / "Hey John, with the recent Crowdstrike outage, we saw just how quick disasters can happen."
- **Direct impact:** Subject: `tariffs` / "Hey John, with cost rising on imports from China, are you considering your options?"
- **Regulation:** Subject: `NIS2 regulations` / "Hey John, with the new NIS2 regulations it's now mandatory to back up your identity provider."

## 3. Value Propositions

### Style 1: Show the Cost of the Problem
`[Observation] > [Here's what that's costing you] > [Specific number]`

```
Hey John, most businesses at the 5-year mark are leaving $50K-$200K
in available capital on the table. Typically because they don't know
what they qualify for.

Based on your revenue range, {{company}} likely qualifies for more
than you'd expect. Would you be open to hearing more?
```

### Style 2: Peer Proof ("Others like you are doing X")
`[Observation] > [Others like you are doing X] > [Result they're getting]`

```
Hey John, congrats on crossing the 5-year mark. A lot of businesses
at your stage are using growth capital to fund expansion without
touching their cash reserves.

One owner in {{industry}} locked in $150K at terms that let him keep
full ownership. Would something like that be useful for {{company}}?
```

### Style 3: Show a Specific Outcome
`[Observation] > [Here's the specific outcome] > [Want to see if it applies?]`

```
Hey John, since you just opened a second location, I'd imagine
managing cash flow across both is a priority.

We recently helped a {{industry}} business secure $200K in growth
capital in under 2 weeks with no equity or collateral.
Would it make sense to see what {{company}} qualifies for?
```

## 4. CTAs

**Interest-Based (safest at scale):**
- "Think this could help your team?"
- "Interested to learn more about this?"
- "Worth exploring?"

**Value-First / Resource:**
- "Want me to send over a quick breakdown?"
- "I put together a comparison for businesses your size — want me to share it?"

**Routing / Right Person:**
- "Are you the right person that handles {{responsibility}} at {{company}}?"
- "Would this sit with you or someone else on the team?"

## Best Templates (Top 10)

### 1. The Mirror
Names the exact situation without pitching. Opens with the tension they feel but haven't articulated.

### 2. Do the Math
For companies with large customer bases. Quantifies the specific dollar impact of the problem.

### 3. The New Exec
Targets new hires in first 90 days. They are actively looking for wins and open to new tools.

### 4. Customer Reconnect
Former champions at new companies. They already know your value — just need the nudge.

### 5. Hiring "End Users"
Triggered by job postings for roles your product serves. The hire means the problem is real.

### 6. Account-Based Framework
Multi-stakeholder enterprise. Different message per role in the buying committee.

### 7. 3rd Party Content Follow-Up
Educational follow-up using relevant content. Adds value without repeating the pitch.

### 8. No-Oriented Question (Re-engagement)
For ghosted threads and no-show demos. "Have you given up on solving X?" — the "no" response reopens the conversation.

### 9. Analogy Email
For complex value props. Uses a relatable analogy to make the problem concrete.

### 10. Job Change (Beanworks Style)
Triggered by role transitions. Acknowledges the change and positions your product as relevant to their new priorities.

## Personalization at Scale

### Level 1: Variable Insertion (table stakes)
`{{firstName}}`, `{{company}}`, `{{industry}}`, `{{city}}`, `{{employee_count}}`

### Level 2: Segment-Level Personalization (the differentiator)
Instead of personalizing per person, personalize per segment:

1. **Enrich** — Use Clay to add company age, employee count, industry, revenue
2. **Segment** — Split by vertical, company size, trigger type, geography
3. **Write variant copy per segment** — Each segment gets:
   - 2-3 subject lines (A/B test)
   - 1 tailored opening
   - 1 tailored value prop
   - 1 universal CTA

**Key insight:** Segment-level personalization has a better effort-to-output ratio than individual personalization. A perfectly targeted segment message outperforms a generically personalized individual message.

## Benchmarks

Judge on replies, never opens (open tracking stays off on cold outbound).

| Metric | Target |
|--------|--------|
| Positive reply rate | 5-8% |
| Meeting book rate | 2-4% |
| Emails per inbox/day | 50-100 max |

### Kill / Keep / Scale Decision

| Metric | Kill | Keep | Scale |
|--------|------|------|-------|
| Positive reply rate | <1% | 2-5% | >5% |
| Emails per positive reply | >5,000 | 1,000-3,000 | <1,000 |

### A/B Testing Rules
- Test 3 subject lines per campaign
- Track reply-to-meeting rate, not just opens
- After 200+ sends per variant, kill underperformers
- 13X gap exists between worst and best variants — testing matters enormously

## Key Principles

1. **70-90 words optimal** — Keep it tight
2. **2-step sequences work best** — Don't over-sequence
3. **Subject + Preview = complete thought** — They work together (first 50 chars appear in inbox)
4. **Soft CTAs > time asks** — "Think this could help?" beats "Got 15 min?"
5. **Segment > individual personalization** — Better effort-to-output ratio at scale
6. **Position yourself as a potential customer** in the subject line
7. **Write for the 97%** — Optimize for the majority who will not reply, not the 3% who will
8. **Follow-ups only work if step 1 was solid** — A bad first email cannot be saved by volume

### Pain-Point Angles (mine them from your own sales calls)

Read your discovery and sales call transcripts and pull the 3-5 situations buyers describe in their own words. Each angle is one line in the buyer's language. Common shapes:

1. **The Ceiling** — "How do you get [outcome] beyond [the channel/method that has stopped scaling]?"
2. **The Failed DIY** — "You tried [doing it yourself], it didn't work"
3. **System vs. Inputs** — "You need [a system], not just more [inputs]"
4. **Scale Without Headcount** — "Grow [outcome] without hiring more [role]"

## Common Mistakes

- Too-long subject lines (7+ words)
- Misleading clickbait
- Same copy for every persona
- ALL CAPS or spammy words ("FREE", "OFFER", "EXCLUSIVE")
- Making it about YOU not them
- "Helping {{company}} with growth" — too obviously a pitch
- "Quick call?" — overused, screams cold email
- Using a signal without the "So What?" test: Is it recent? Does it connect to my offer? Would THEY care?
