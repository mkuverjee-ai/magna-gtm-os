# Sequence structures

The shapes we use. Two or three steps only. The three-step is the default. Use the two-step when the offer is simple enough to make the case in one email plus a breakup.

## Three-step (default)

**Email 1, core context.** The seller.
- Open on the prospect's pain or a shift in their world that makes now the moment.
- State the wedge: the specific thing you can do about it.
- One low-friction ask.
- 75 to 90 words.

**Email 2, the platform.** The wingman.
- Elaborate on what the product does beyond email 1's hook.
- Show how it makes their day easier, concretely.
- Additive, not a repeat. Keep it tight.
- One ask.

**Email 3, the breakup.** The closer.
- "This is the last time I am reaching out."
- "If now is not the time, tell me and I am happy to circle back."
- "If you are not the right person, point me to who is."
- Short. This captures the procrastinators who now know it is their last chance.

## Two-step

**Email 1, core context.** Same as above.
**Email 2, the breakup.** Same as above, folding in one line of value from what would have been email 2.

Use this when the value is simple and a middle email would only pad.

## Worked skeleton (fictional company, Loopwise, a finance-ops automation SaaS)

Angle: finance teams close the books manually and it eats the last week of every month.

- **Email 1.** Open on the month-end crunch they know too well. Wedge: Loopwise automates the reconciliations that eat that week. Ask: worth a look?
- **Email 2.** What Loopwise actually does day to day, and the hours it gives back. Ask: open to a short walk-through?
- **Email 3.** Breakup. Last touch, circle-back offer, or point me to the right person.

The skeleton is the frame. The copy comes from the prospect's real pain, the research wedge, and the copy rules. Never ship the skeleton as copy.
