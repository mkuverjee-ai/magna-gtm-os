# Exemplar sequences

Worked examples the skill can pattern-match against. Every example uses a fictional company so nothing real is exposed. The structures below are distilled from the highest-performing sequences in a store of 809 campaigns with positive replies and rewritten onto fictional companies.

## Structural exemplar · Loopwise (finance-ops automation SaaS)

Angle: finance teams close the books by hand and it eats the last week of every month.

Subject: your month-end crunch

Loopwise automates the reconciliations that eat that week. Same data, same controls, a fraction of the hours.

One team cut their close from nine days to four in the first month.

Worth a quick look at whether it fits how you close?

Subject: how it actually works

Hi [First name],

Quick follow-up on the month-end point.

Loopwise connects to your ledger, matches transactions automatically, and flags only the exceptions your team needs to touch. No rip-and-replace. It sits on top of what you already run.

The result is the close done in days, not the last miserable week of the month, with a clean audit trail.

Open to a short walk-through this week or next?

## From the campaign store (anonymized)

Real structural patterns from top performers, rewritten onto fictional companies.

### Pattern A · the personalized-idea opener

One of the highest-reply shapes in the book. Lead with specific, per-lead ideas, then scale the offer. (Fictional company: Reflow, a marketing-ops agency.)

Subject: Reflow <> [Company]

Hey [First name], thanks for engaging with our content.

We analyzed [Company] and came up with three plays you could run this quarter:

1. [Idea 1]
2. [Idea 2]
3. [Idea 3]

Now imagine sending your own prospects three tailored ideas like this, at scale.

Worth exploring what that would look like for [Company]?

[Signature]

Why it works: the three custom ideas prove you did the work before you asked for anything. The custom lines are generated per lead, so QC the merged output before send.

### Pattern B · social proof carries the length

The winners that run past 100 words earn it with a real number and a real name. (Fictional company: Palette, a creative-as-a-service brand.)

Subject: {{RANDOM| design help without hiring | your creative bottleneck | design team stretched thin?}}

{{RANDOM|Hi|Hey}} [First name],

Reaching out to see if your team could use extra design support.

Palette plugs in as your remote design team: fast, on-brand creative without adding headcount. Digital ads, social, decks, whatever your roadmap calls for.

Over 4,000 growth teams, including two names you would recognize, run their creative through us.

Any interest in a quick look?

[Signature]

P.S. If this isn't relevant, just say so and I'll leave you alone.

Why it works: the spintax subject varies every send, the social-proof line justifies the extra words, and the P.S. gives a graceful exit that still counts as a reply.

### Pattern C · the free-value follow-up

A middle-step move that reactivates quiet threads by giving instead of asking. (Fictional company: Bridgewell, an integrations platform.)

Hi [First name],

Totally get that teams like [Company] want to assess this internally first.

If any part of it is worth a second look, would it help if I sent over our integration playbook? It is built from work with 300+ teams.

Happy to share, no strings.

[Signature]

Why it works: it offers a resource, not a meeting. The ask is tiny, so the reply is cheap.

## Why these hold up

- Spintax on subjects and openers, so no two sends are identical.
- Social proof (a number plus a name) placed after the value prop and before the ask.
- One soft-question CTA per email.
- The breakup offers a menu, which converts the procrastinators.
- Every merged field gets QC'd before send, because one failed line poisons the whole email.
