# Copy rules, and why each one holds

The rules the skill enforces on every email, with the reason behind each. Reasons matter because they tell you when a rule bends and when it does not.

## Length

- **75 to 90 words per email.** Cold email is read on a phone, between other things. Past 90 words it gets skimmed, then deleted. Under 75 it usually means you skipped the value.
- **Sentences under 20 words.** Long sentences lose a busy reader mid-thought. Short sentences are also easier for non-native English readers, who are a large share of many campaigns.

## Structure

- **Two or three steps, never more.** If you cannot say what you do and the value in two or three emails, the offer or the targeting is broken. Four and five step sequences stretch the content thin and the extra steps stop adding value. They also wear out the prospect.
- **One ask per email.** Two CTAs split the decision and lower the odds of either. Pick the single next step.

## Craft

- **Lead with the pain or the wedge.** Not the product, not "I hope this finds you well." The first two lines decide whether the rest gets read. Open on something true about the prospect's world.
- **No em dashes.** A house style rule, and a tell. Rewrite to flow with commas and periods.
- **Simple language for non-native and non-technical audiences.** Short words, no idioms, no jargon. If a sentence needs a second read, cut it down.
- **Specific beats clever.** A concrete detail the prospect recognizes about themselves beats a clever line they have seen before.

## Infrastructure and metrics (never break these)

- **Reply-based metrics only.** Judge campaigns on replies, positive replies, and meetings booked. Never on opens or clicks.
- **No open or click tracking on cold outbound.** Tracking pixels and rewritten links hurt inbox placement. Do not add them, do not recommend them, and do not frame their absence as a visibility gap.

## The failure mode to always check for

Copy generated at scale can silently break. A personalized research line produced across thousands of rows will, on some rows, return a non-answer like "I do not have access to the internet." It ships as the most important line of the email if nobody checks. Always QC the merged output, not just the template.
