# Subject lines

Two learnings that hold across hundreds of campaigns. Treat both as strong A/B-test defaults, not laws.

## Name the pain in three to five words

Short subject lines that reference the actual pain outperform, especially when the body has a well-developed version of that same pain. The subject is a promise. The body keeps it. When they match, the open turns into a read.

Weak: "Quick question about your outbound"
Better: "buyers going quiet?"
Better: "missing your best-fit accounts"

## The word "your"

Including "your" in the subject line has lifted response rates by roughly 15 percent across campaigns, when the pain framing is strong and specific. It makes the line about the reader, not the sender.

"your" works because it points the pain at them. It does nothing if the pain itself is generic. The lift comes from the pairing.

## How to use these

- Write three to five subject lines per email, half with "your" and half without, all naming a specific pain in three to five words.
- Keep them lowercase or sentence case, never title case or all caps. Title case reads like marketing.
- A/B test. What wins in one market or persona will not always win in another.

## What to avoid

- Curiosity-gap clickbait ("you won't believe..."). It opens, then the body disappoints, and the reply never comes.
- Anything that reads like a newsletter subject.
- Fake "Re:" or "Fwd:" prefixes. They open at the cost of trust, and trust is what earns the reply.
