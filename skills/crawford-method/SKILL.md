# The Crawford Method

## The Master Principle

> "The list is the message. If you have a good list, you have a good message."

Targeting IS messaging. Build the list on data criteria that reflect the buyer's actual situation and tension. Then describe their situation back to them. The question is never "what should I write?" but "which buyer gets 10x more value from us, and what situation are they in?"

---

## Two Message Types

### PQS — Pain-Qualified Segment

Uses 2-5 public data heuristics to identify prospects in the same painful situation as an existing customer, then tells them the story of how your customers navigated that situation.

**Formula:** (1) Name the obvious choice everyone makes. (2) Explain why your customers made the non-obvious choice. (3) Show the benefit of the non-obvious choice.

PQS is about your product solving their pain. It says "I see you're in this situation. We've solved it before."

**Use when:** You have clear customer stories that map to identifiable data heuristics, and the tension is high enough that situational awareness alone earns a reply.

### PVP — Permissionless Value Prop (Always Superior)

Structures public data to provide a non-obvious insight that is independently valuable, related to your product area, and compiled only from public data.

**The acid test:** Would the recipient pay to receive this message, even if they never buy from you? If yes, it is a PVP. If the value disappears without your product, it is a PQS.

**How it works:** Blend 3-5 public data sources. The individual data points are all public. The SYNTHESIS is what makes it valuable. The message delivers the insight directly with no pitch needed. Starting a conversation is the side effect.

| | PQS | PVP |
|---|---|---|
| About | Your product solving their pain | Intelligence they need regardless of you |
| Value | "We understand your situation" | "Here's something you can use right now" |
| Pitch | Implicit | None |
| Strength | Good | Best |

**Use PVP whenever you can.** Especially powerful for vertical SaaS, when you can deploy AI agents to generate per-prospect insights, or when you have access to interesting data sources (permits, filings, ad libraries, tech stacks).

## The Tension Framework

Tension is the specific, data-provable, time-bound situation that makes a prospect need what you sell RIGHT NOW. Tension is NOT a single signal. Signals are one data point. Tension requires multiple data points pulling in different directions.

### The Tension Triangle

```
        [Growth Amplifier]
             /\
            /  \
           /    \
[Heuristic 1] --- [Heuristic 2]
```

1. **Heuristic 1** — A unique, data-provable characteristic
2. **Heuristic 2** — A second characteristic that PULLS AGAINST the first
3. **Growth Amplifier** — Revenue growth, funding, headcount growth that increases the pull

### Examples

**Month-End Crunch (finance automation, illustrative):**
- H1: Finance team headcount flat for 12+ months (no new hires to absorb load)
- H2: New legal entities or locations added in the same period (transaction volume rising)
- Growth: Recent funding creating pressure to report faster and cleaner

**CS Disco (eDiscovery):**
- H1: Company facing a substantial lawsuit (court filing data)
- H2: Company is in an industry where litigation is INFREQUENT (never solved for this)
- Growth: Electronic records multiplying costs (3-5x data volume)

**WingWork (Aviation):**
- H1: Company owns aircraft affected by upcoming FAA regulation
- H2: Number of affected planes quantifies the cost
- Growth: Regulation deadline creates time pressure

### The Existential Data Point

The single metric that separates good-fit from bad-fit. Too low = bad fit (already solved). Too high = bad fit (failing business). Sweet spot = maximum tension.

### How to Identify Tension

1. Start with best customers. What was their situation when they bought?
2. Identify 2-3 data points true about them AT TIME OF PURCHASE
3. Can you find these programmatically from public sources?
4. Look for the PULL — which two data points create opposing forces?
5. Define the existential data point and its sweet spot range
6. Build the list on these criteria, NOT firmographic filters

## 7-Component Email Architecture

### 1. Visceral Emotional Opening
Start with a moment they have physically experienced. Not a fact. A FEELING.

- GOOD: "Did your dishwasher no-show again?"
- GOOD: "What happens to your close when two more entities land next quarter?"
- BAD: "I noticed you recently raised a Series A."
- BAD: "As a CEO in the technology sector..."

### 2. Connection to Existing Behavior
Frame your offer as an extension of something they already do. "What if you could outsource your dish pit?" — they already outsource linen. Deliberately NOT saying "dish robot" because that is a new concept.

### 3. The Insight (Contrarian Structure)
> "Most people think X, but it turns out Y."

Example: "Most finance teams think a faster close means more people. It turns out the last week of the month is mostly matching, not judgment. Add people and you add more hands doing the matching."

Must pass: (1) Recognition test — they nod and think "that's right." (2) Articulation test — they have felt this but never said it.

### 4. Social Proof / Authority (Light Touch)
Credentials of the founder, not the company. One line.

- GOOD: "Formerly Google's youngest employee and 10 years in robotics."
- BAD: "We've helped 500+ companies increase pipeline by 40%."

### 5. Information Gap
Say enough to create curiosity, never enough to answer all questions.

- GOOD: "We build a robot to wash your dishes on site." (How? I need to know more.)
- BAD: "We build an automated robotic system using AI-powered arms to clean 500 dishes/hour at $X/month." (All questions answered.)

### 6. Qualification as Disqualification
Include a threshold that excludes non-fits. Qualified readers feel the message is specifically for them.

- "Do you have 5 hours a day of dishwashing?"
- "Not every layout works for us."
- "If it's still working great, ignore me."

### 7. Soft CTA (Ask for Truth, Not Time)

**Diagnostic:** "Am I close, or is it different at {{companyName}}?" / "Ring any bells?"
**Value-offer:** "Want me to send the playbook?" / "Want the complete list with their new titles?"
**Physical:** "Can I swing by, check out your kitchen and show you?"

**NEVER use:** "Do you have 15 minutes?" / "Book a time on my calendar" / "Quick call?"

## 11 Copywriting Rules

1. **Never mention your product in step 1.** Remove your company name — does the email still provide value?
2. **Write about their situation, not your solution.** Bridge the gap by proving you understand, not by pitching.
3. **Target based on events, not roles.** "VP Marketing" tells you nothing. "VP Marketing whose company just got hit with an FAA regulation" tells you everything.
4. **Use Cialdini systematically.** Unity (insider language), Authority (founder credentials), Scarcity ("not every layout works"), Social Proof (others in same situation), Commitment (small yes first), Reciprocity (give value first).
5. **Create information gaps, don't close them.** The email starts conversations, not closes deals.
6. **Use their language.** "Hop on dish" is how restaurant people talk. Find phrases from call transcripts, Reddit, forums.
7. **Sell against incumbent behavior, not competitors.** "Cheaper than your people" not "better than CompetitorX."
8. **Acknowledge multiple solutions exist.** "There are many ways to handle this, here's what an expert would do" builds more trust than "our product is best."
9. **One perfect email > ten mediocre ones.** If you cannot write one perfect message to one perfect prospect by hand, you cannot scale outbound.
10. **Data-driven > personalization.** Dollar amounts, permit numbers, competitor analysis. NOT "I saw your LinkedIn post."
11. **Quantify the meeting.** Put a dollar amount on the value of responding: "$62M in untapped potential" / "$8 million on cables alone."

## Real Examples

### Dish Robot (PQS — Visceral Opening)
**Subject:** Did your dishwasher no-show again?
```
What if you could outsource your dish pit?

[Founder] has been building robots for 10 years,
formerly Google's youngest employee.

We build a robot to wash your dishes on site.
And it's cheaper than your people.

Not every layout works for us.
Can I swing by, check out your kitchen and show you?
```
Why it works: Visceral opening, anchors to existing behavior (outsourcing), sells against labor, scarcity via qualification, physical CTA. Zero personalization variables. Zero product jargon.

### Texada Equipment Rental (PVP — The Gold Standard)
```
Earl, your CAT 365 excavator seems to be not on the move right now.

Did you see that they just filed a large construction project for
123 Main Street? It's about a half a mile away from you.

I threw this thing at AI and it says that it probably is going to
need your tool for about six weeks.

Looking at public rental rates, that's about a $170,000 job.

Here is the person to contact. Here's the foreman.
Here's their email. Here's their phone number.

I hope this was useful.
```
Why this is the gold standard: Independently valuable (Earl gets a qualified lead with contact info, dollar value, timeline). No product mention. Three public data sources synthesized (DOT permits, construction permits, rental rates). Quantified. The message IS the product demo.

### CS Disco eDiscovery (PQS — Quantified Tension)
```
Your initial irrelevant data volume could expand three to five times,
potentially driving outside counsel review costs towards
$5 million to $13 million.

Are you anticipating these discovery costs to start becoming
significant within the next three to six months?
```
Why it works: Three sentences. Quantified range ($5-13M feels more credible than single number). Time anchor creates urgency. No product mentioned.

## The Process: Zero to Finished Email

1. **Understand best customers.** Get CEO, exec, 1-2 salespeople in a room with call transcripts. Ask: "What was the situation when they bought?"
2. **Identify tension.** Extract Heuristic 1, Heuristic 2, Growth Amplifier, Existential Data Point.
3. **Find data sources.** Can you find heuristics in public/programmatic data? (Government filings, job postings, tech stacks, LinkedIn, reviews, ad libraries, conferences, funding databases)
4. **Build list on tension, not firmographics.** List criteria = tension heuristics, NOT revenue ranges and employee counts.
5. **Write backwards.** Define the perfect message, then ask what data you need to make it true, then check if you can find that data programmatically.
6. **Choose PQS or PVP.** PVP if you can synthesize public data into independently valuable insight. PQS if value comes from your expertise/story.
7. **Apply the 7-component architecture.**
8. **Run the quality checklist:**
   - Does this describe their situation, not our product?
   - Can I remove our company name and it still provides value?
   - Are there 2+ heuristics creating opposing pull?
   - Does the opening trigger a physical memory?
   - Would someone in their industry recognize the language?
   - Are there specific numbers or dollar amounts?
   - Am I asking for truth, not asking for time?
   - Does the email raise questions it doesn't answer?

## Anti-Patterns

- **"I noticed" opening** — Fake observation, everyone sees press releases
- **"Companies like yours"** — Zero specificity, means "I don't know you"
- **"Do you have 15 minutes"** — Asking for time before earning it
- **Features dump** — "Our solutions streamline processes" (what solutions?)
- **Surface personalization** — Location, mutual connections, LinkedIn posts fill space where insight should be
- **Horizontal targeting** — When your list could be anyone's list, your message cannot demonstrate understanding
- **Over-sequencing** — Max 2-3 touches. Each must add new information, not repeat the pitch.
